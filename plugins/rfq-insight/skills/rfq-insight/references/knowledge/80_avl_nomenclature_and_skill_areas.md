# AVL nomenclature & skill areas (official internal definitions)

These AVL-internal meanings override generic industry usage.

## Nomenclature

- **RFQ** — request for quotation; no standard format.
- **Opportunity** — a potential customer project (RFQ received, offer
  submitted).
- **Phases** — development GENERATIONS from prototype up to volume production;
  not a workstream, a timing-plan line item or a meeting.
- **Variant** — product development for different target applications;
  differentiation in the lead product and related derivative variants.
- **Supplier** — for the customer, AVL and/or third parties; in the report,
  always a THIRD-PARTY project participant (AVL is named AVL).
- **Product** — the product to be developed (combustion engine, electrified
  transmission, battery, powertrain, vehicle, …).
- **Application** — the target environment for the developed product, e.g. the
  target vehicle for a high-voltage battery under development.
- **Location** — where development happens: on-site at the customer,
  off-site at AVL, on extreme-ambient test trips.
- **Release recommendation** — AVL prepares deliverables to allow a customer
  review and customer FINAL release; final release sits with the product-
  responsible party (production of product), generally NOT AVL. Read RFQ
  release/approval clauses with this split in mind.
- **Unclear data** — contradictions, vague, or ambiguous content. This is the
  official name for what the report's Risk Detection lists as document risks.
- **Skill/Task** — a development activity: design, simulation, procurement,
  testing, software development, calibration, production engineering, …

## Skill areas (Operations & Technology, in force since 1 November 2025)

Eleven skill areas pool AVL's technical expertise; each area is split into skill
teams (team code = area code + one letter). Use them to map the RFQ's tasks onto
Scope-of-Work rows (see Mapping rules).

## Mapping rules

1. One Scope-of-Work row per skill area actually in scope, in the prefix form
   `<Area> (<code>): <task>`; split an area into two rows only where ownership
   genuinely differs inside it (e.g. design owned by AVL but 2D drawings by the
   customer, or software split between AVL and a base-software supplier). If you
   are producing 25+ rows, you are transcribing the RFQ's task list instead of
   mapping it to skill areas.
2. Name the skill team inside the task text only when the RFQ's wording
   identifies it, e.g. `Validation & Industrialization (DM): FMEA, supplier
   nomination and PPAP (DMC)`. A skill team never gets a row of its own.
3. Commercial-vehicle scope (truck, bus, off-road, commercial driveline) goes to
   Commercial Driveline & Vehicle (DK), whatever the task type; DK's teams cover
   functional development, design, E/E and calibration, and testing for these
   products. A heavy-duty engine developed on its own, outside a
   commercial-vehicle program, stays in its functional area (e.g. heavy-duty
   engine calibration in DC).
4. Project management goes to Program & Project Management (DP) only when the
   RFQ states it or clearly implies it (a project-manager role, PM deliverables,
   reporting duties); never add a DP row by default.
5. Research & Technology Development (DR) and Intellectual Property (DI) rarely
   carry RFQ scope: no row unless the RFQ names research or IP work as a task.
6. These areas set the Scope-of-Work row granularity only. Opportunity Info's
   Skills (%) uses the Guidance Document's skill list in
   90_guidance_denominators.md (Functional Development, Simulation, Design, …),
   never these names.
